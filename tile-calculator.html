How to Calculate Tiles for Floors and Walls

Calculating the correct amount of tiles prevents shortages mid-project or overspending on excess materials. Here is how tile estimation works:

  1. Measure the length and width of the room and multiply them to get the total surface area.
  2. Calculate the area of a single tile by multiplying its length and width.
  3. Divide the room area by the individual tile area to find the base number of tiles.
  4. Add a waste percentage (usually 10% for straight layouts, 15% to 20% for diagonal cuts or rooms with pillars) to account for cuts and breakages.

The Complete Tile Quantity Guide: Formulas, Examples & Tips

Learn exactly how to calculate how many tiles you need for any room — including waste, patterns, and ordering strategies — so your project never runs short.

The Tile Quantity Formula Explained

The basic tile quantity calculation is a two-step process. First, calculate the room area. Second, divide by the coverage of one tile, then apply a waste allowance:

Room Area = Length × Width (in the same unit)
Base Tile Count = Room Area ÷ Tile Area
Total with Waste = Base Count × (1 + Waste% ÷ 100)
Boxes Required = ⌈Total Tiles ÷ Tiles per Box⌉ (round up)

Always convert measurements to a consistent unit before calculating. If your room is in feet but your tile is in inches, convert the tile dimensions to feet first (divide inches by 12).

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1: Bathroom Floor (60×60 cm tiles)

Room: 3.2 m × 2.8 m | Tile: 60 cm × 60 cm (0.6 m × 0.6 m) | Waste: 10%

  1. Room area = 3.2 × 2.8 = 8.96 m²
  2. Tile area = 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36 m²
  3. Base count = 8.96 ÷ 0.36 = 24.9 → 25 tiles
  4. With 10% waste: 25 × 1.10 = 27.5 → 28 tiles
  5. If tiles come 6 per box: 28 ÷ 6 = 4.67 → 5 boxes

Example 2: Living Room Floor (12×12 inch tiles)

Room: 15 ft × 12 ft | Tile: 12 in × 12 in = 1 ft × 1 ft | Waste: 10%

  1. Room area = 15 × 12 = 180 sq ft
  2. Tile area = 1 × 1 = 1 sq ft
  3. Base count = 180 ÷ 1 = 180 tiles
  4. With 10% waste: 180 × 1.10 = 198 tiles
  5. At 10 tiles per box: 198 ÷ 10 = 20 boxes

Example 3: Kitchen Backsplash (Diagonal Pattern)

Wall: 4 ft × 3 ft = 12 sq ft | Tile: 4×4 inch subway tiles | Diagonal pattern waste: 15%

  1. Tile area in sq ft = (4/12) × (4/12) = 0.333 × 0.333 = 0.111 sq ft
  2. Base count = 12 ÷ 0.111 = 108 tiles
  3. With 15% diagonal waste: 108 × 1.15 = 124.2 → 125 tiles

Recommended Waste Percentages by Installation Type

The right waste allowance depends on the layout complexity, tile size, room shape, and installer experience:

Installation Type Recommended Waste Reason
Straight grid (regular room)8–10%Minimal cuts, predictable edges
Staggered / brick bond10–12%More edge cuts needed
Diagonal (45°) pattern15–20%Large corner triangles create waste
Herringbone pattern15–20%Complex angled cuts throughout
Large-format tiles (60cm+)10–15%Breakage risk; fewer but costly cuts
L-shaped or complex rooms15%+Multiple re-cut pieces required

Practical Buying & Installation Tips

  • Buy from one lot: Tiles from different production batches (dye lots) can have subtle color or size variations that are only visible when laid side by side. Always check the batch number on each box.
  • Dry lay first: Before applying adhesive, lay a row of tiles across the longest dimension to check cuts and centering. Adjust the starting line to avoid narrow slivers at the edges.
  • Keep spares: Store at least 3–5% of tiles after installation, labeled with the product code and lot number. Future repairs are nearly impossible if the tile is discontinued.
  • Verify tile dimensions: Nominal tile sizes (e.g., "60×60") may differ from actual calibrated dimensions. Check the technical datasheet for exact measurements before calculating.
  • Check the substrate: Large-format tiles require a flatter substrate (maximum 3mm deviation in 2 meters). Ensure the floor or wall is properly prepared to prevent cracking.
