How to Calculate Tiles for Floors and Walls

Calculating the correct amount of tiles prevents shortages mid-project or overspending on excess materials. Here is how tile estimation works:

  1. Measure the length and width of the room and multiply them to get the total surface area.
  2. Calculate the area of a single tile by multiplying its length and width.
  3. Divide the room area by the individual tile area to find the base number of tiles.
  4. Add a waste percentage (usually 10% for straight layouts, 15% to 20% for diagonal cuts or rooms with pillars) to account for cuts and breakages.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate how many tiles I need? ▼

To calculate how many tiles you need, first measure the length and width of your room to get the total square footage. Then divide by the area of one tile. Add 10-15% extra for waste and cuts. Our free tile calculator does all of this automatically.

What waste margin should I add for tiles? ▼

For most projects, add 10% for waste. For diagonal patterns or rooms with many corners, add 15%. For complex mosaic patterns, consider 20%. Our calculator includes a customizable waste margin.

Can this calculator be used for wall tiles? ▼

Yes, our tile calculator works for both floor tiles and wall tiles. Simply enter the dimensions of your wall area instead of floor area.

Why should I add a waste percentage? ▼

Cutting tiles to fit corners, edges, and pipes inevitably results in discarded scraps and occasional breakage. Adding 10% extra ensures you won't run short.

How do I measure irregular rooms? ▼

Break down complex or L-shaped rooms into smaller rectangular sections, calculate the area of each section separately, and add them together.

Assumptions and formula

Room area is length × width. Tile area is tile length × tile width. The basic tile count is room area divided by tile area, then rounded up; the actual layout may need more because a cut piece cannot always be reused. Diagonal, herringbone, staggered, and multi-size patterns often create more offcuts than a straight grid.

Buying and installation checks

Confirm the tile is rated for the intended wall or floor, check water absorption and slip resistance, and buy boxes from the same lot when color matters. Dry-lay several rows, inspect the narrow cuts at edges, and keep a spare box labeled with the lot number. Check the substrate, grout joint, adhesive, and manufacturer instructions before installation.

Mistakes and limitations

Do not confuse inches with feet, forget a wall height, treat waste as usable area, or assume every package has the same coverage. Tile Quantity Formula

Calculate room area first, then divide by the coverage of one tile. A 12 by 24 inch tile covers 288 square inches, or 2 square feet. For a 120-square-foot room, 120 ÷ 2 = 60 tiles before waste. With 10% allowance, 60 × 1.10 = 66 tiles. Round up to a whole tile and then to complete boxes because an opened box may not be returnable.

Layout, grout, and ordering

Tile dimensions are nominal; verify the actual product coverage and recommended grout joint. Dry-lay a row to see where cuts fall, especially around doors, cabinets, drains, and corners. Straight layouts commonly use 10% waste, while diagonal and patterned layouts may need 15% or more. Buy the same dye lot, save the product details, and keep spare pieces for repairs.

Common Mistakes and FAQs

Do not multiply the room perimeter, mix inches with feet, or subtract uncertain openings. Should waste be added before division? No, calculate theoretical tiles first, then apply the allowance. What if two rooms use the same tile? Calculate each layout separately, add the results, and consider keeping extra from one lot. Box and Layout Checklist

Check the tile’s stated coverage, box quantity, caliber, shade, and recommended joint. Measure the longest run and the smallest edge piece before committing to a centered layout. A narrow cut may be avoided by moving the reference line a few inches. Include threshold transitions and movement joints where the installation instructions require them.

After installation

Store spare tiles with the product name and lot information. Photograph hidden plumbing or underfloor details before covering them. Keep the final calculation and receipts with the maintenance records in case a future repair needs a matching batch.

Pattern Allowance

Large-format tile, offset patterns, niches, and diagonal cuts often create more offcuts than a simple grid. Measure the usable wall or floor after framing, allow for grout, and confirm that the substrate is ready. Order enough from one production lot for the complete visible area.

Keep the record

Save a box label, layout sketch, and final quantity. These details make future repairs faster when a product is discontinued.

Worked tile example

A 12 by 15 ft room contains 180 square feet. For 12 by 12 inch tiles, each tile covers 1 square foot before waste, so a simple estimate is 180 tiles. A 10% allowance raises this to 198 tiles. If a box contains 10 tiles, buy 20 boxes. For a wall, calculate each wall rectangle and subtract doors or windows that will not be tiled.

Steps that prevent shortages

  1. Measure length and width at several points and use the largest practical dimension.
  2. Convert all measurements to one unit before finding area.
  3. Choose a waste rate: about 10% for a simple straight layout and more for diagonal or patterned work.
  4. Check tile coverage per box, lot or dye number, and return policy before ordering.
