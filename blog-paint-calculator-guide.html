Paint Calculator Guide — How Much Paint Do You Really Need?

By Alex Morgan · August 2026 · 10 min read

You've picked out the perfect color, taped off the trim, and laid down the drop cloths. But before you open that first can, the most crucial question arises: how much paint do I need? Using a reliable paint calculator is essential. Guessing too low means rushing back to the store mid-project (and risking a mismatched color batch). Guessing too high leaves you with expensive leftover cans gathering dust in the garage. In this comprehensive paint buying guide, we will break down exactly how to calculate paint for a room so you can start painting with confidence.

The Basic Paint Formula

At the core of every wall paint estimator is a straightforward mathematical equation. To figure out your required volume, you just need to know your surface area, the number of coats, and the paint coverage rate.

Total Paint = (Wall Area × Coats) ÷ Coverage Rate
  • Wall Area: The total square footage (or square meters) you are painting, minus unpainted areas like doors and windows.
  • Coats: How many layers of paint you plan to apply (usually 2).
  • Coverage Rate: How much area one unit of paint covers (e.g., paint coverage per gallon or per liter).

Step-by-Step: Measuring Your Room

Before you plug numbers into the paint calculator, you need accurate measurements.

Step 1: Calculate the Perimeter

Measure the length of each wall in the room and add them together. For example, in a rectangular room that is 4 meters by 3 meters, the perimeter is: 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 = 14 meters.

Step 2: Multiply by Height

Measure the wall height from the floor (or baseboard) to the ceiling. Multiply the perimeter by the height. If the ceiling is 2.8 meters high: 14m × 2.8m = 39.2 square meters of total wall space.

Step 3: Subtract Doors and Windows

You aren't painting over the glass! A standard door is about 2 m² and a standard window is about 1.5 m². Subtract these from your total wall area.

Quick Tip:

If you plan to paint the doors and trim as well, calculate them separately using the coverage rate for trim enamel, which differs from standard wall paint.

Worked Example

Let's use a real-world scenario to calculate paint for a room. We have our 4m × 3m room with a 2.8m ceiling. It has 2 standard doors and 2 standard windows.

  • Total Gross Area: 14m perimeter × 2.8m height = 39.2 m²
  • Subtract Doors: 2 doors × 2 m² = 4 m²
  • Subtract Windows: 2 windows × 1.5 m² = 3 m²
  • Net Paintable Area: 39.2 - 4 - 3 = 32.2 m²

Assuming a typical coverage of 10 m²/L and doing 2 coats:

(32.2 m² × 2 coats) ÷ 10 m²/L = 6.44 Liters.

You would need to buy 7 liters of paint.

Coverage Rates Explained

Not all paints cover equally. The standard paint coverage per gallon is roughly 350 to 400 square feet (about 10 m² per liter), but several factors can change this:

  • Surface Texture: Rough, textured walls (like stucco or heavy knockdown) absorb more paint and can reduce coverage by up to 20%.
  • Paint Quality: Premium brands often contain higher volumes of titanium dioxide (the pigment that hides old color), providing better coverage.
  • Finish/Sheen: Flat paints tend to hide imperfections better on the first coat, while high-gloss paints spread further but might require more coats to look even.

How Many Coats Do You Need?

The biggest variable in your wall paint estimator is the number of coats. Use this quick guide:

1 Coat (Rare)

Painting over a similar color with a high-quality "paint and primer in one."

2 Coats (Standard)

The general rule for most interior walls to achieve true color and durability.

3+ Coats (Specialty)

Painting light colors over dark walls, or using vibrant reds, yellows, and deep blues.

New Drywall

Always requires a dedicated primer coat first because the surface is highly porous.

Buying the Right Amount

Once your paint calculator gives you the final number, follow these golden rules for buying:

Always Round Up: If your calculation says 2.2 gallons, buy 3 gallons. If it says 6.4 liters, buy a 10-liter tub or 7 individual liters. Running out is a nightmare.

Add a Waste Buffer: Always add a 5-10% buffer to your final number to account for spills, roller absorption, and future touch-ups.

Warning: Batch Numbers Matter

When buying multiple cans of custom-mixed color, check that the batch numbers on the lids match, or mix all the cans together in a large bucket before starting (called "boxing") to ensure absolute color consistency.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Forgetting the Ceiling

Ceilings require specific ceiling paint. Do not forget to calculate the ceiling area (length × width) separately!

❌ Mixing Up Units

Ensure you aren't measuring your room in feet but looking at coverage rates in square meters. Keep your units consistent.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much paint do I need for a 12x12 room?

Assuming 8-foot ceilings, a 12x12 room has 384 square feet of wall space. Minus a door and window, you have about 350 sq ft. For two coats, you'll need roughly 2 gallons of paint.

Does a gallon really cover 400 square feet?

Under perfect laboratory conditions, yes. In reality, on average, expect a gallon to cover closer to 350 square feet on smooth walls, and even less on textured surfaces or raw drywall.

How long does leftover paint last?

Properly sealed and stored in a cool, dry place (not a freezing garage), latex paint can last up to 10 years, and oil-based paint up to 15 years.
