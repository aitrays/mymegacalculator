Understanding Body Mass Index (BMI)

Body Mass Index (BMI) is an internationally recognized screening metric used by the World Health Organization (WHO) and healthcare providers to evaluate whether an adult has a healthy body weight for their height.

Category BMI Range (kg/m²) Health Implication
Underweight < 18.5 Possible nutritional deficiency or underlying health issue
Normal Weight 18.5 – 24.9 Lowest risk of weight-related health conditions
Overweight 25.0 – 29.9 Moderate risk of cardiovascular disease and hypertension
Obesity (Class 1) 30.0 – 34.9 Increased risk for metabolic syndrome and diabetes
Obesity (Class 2 & 3) 35.0+ High risk of chronic diseases and cardiovascular complications

BMI Calculation Formulas

Metric Formula:

BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²

Example: 70 kg / (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.86 kg/m²

Imperial Formula:

BMI = 703 × Weight (lbs) / [Height (in)]²

Example: 703 × 154 / (69 × 69) = 22.74

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a normal BMI range? ▼

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), a normal BMI for adults is between 18.5 and 24.9. A BMI below 18.5 is considered underweight, 25.0 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30.0 or higher indicates obesity.

How is BMI calculated? ▼

For metric units, BMI is calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared: BMI = kg / (m²). For imperial units, the formula is BMI = (weight in lbs × 703) / (height in inches²).

What is the healthy weight range for my height? ▼

A healthy weight range corresponds to a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9. Our calculator automatically computes your minimum and maximum healthy weight in both kilograms and pounds based on your entered height.

Is BMI accurate for athletes and bodybuilders? ▼

BMI does not distinguish between muscle mass and fat mass. Because muscle is denser than fat, muscular individuals and athletes may have a high BMI without having excess body fat. It remains a reliable screening tool for the general population.
