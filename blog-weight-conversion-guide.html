Weight Conversion Chart and Guide for Cooking, Fitness, and Travel

By Alex Morgan · August 2026 · 8 min read

Weight is one of the most commonly converted measurements in daily life. Whether you are following a European recipe that lists ingredients in grams, checking your luggage before an international flight, or tracking your gym progress in pounds versus kilograms, knowing how to convert between weight units saves time, money, and frustration. This guide covers everything you need to know about weight conversions, from the basic formulas to practical reference charts and real-world scenarios.

The Two Main Systems: Metric and Imperial

The world uses two primary systems for measuring weight. The metric system (grams, kilograms) is used by almost every country except the United States, Myanmar, and Liberia. The imperial system (ounces, pounds, stones) is still dominant in the US and used informally in the UK.

This divide creates daily friction. A British person talking about "losing a stone" confuses Americans. An American baker trying to follow a French recipe struggles with gram measurements. Understanding both systems is no longer optional — it is essential.

Cooking and Baking Conversions

Cooking is where weight conversions matter most for precision. A cup of flour can weigh anywhere from 120g to 150g depending on how it is packed. Professional bakers always use weight measurements for consistency.

  • 1 cup of all-purpose flour ˜ 120–130 grams
  • 1 cup of granulated sugar ˜ 200 grams
  • 1 tablespoon of butter ˜ 14 grams
  • 1 pound of ground beef = 453.6 grams
  • 1 ounce of chocolate = 28.35 grams

When converting recipes, remember that volume (cups, tablespoons) and weight (grams, ounces) measure different properties. A cup of feathers and a cup of lead occupy the same volume but have wildly different weights. Always use weight for dry ingredients when precision matters.

Fitness and Body Weight

Gym equipment and fitness tracking apps often use different units depending on the country. A barbell loaded with "two 20kg plates" weighs 40kg total (88.2 lbs). If your fitness app tracks in pounds but your gym uses kilogram plates, confusion is common.

Body weight tip: When tracking weight loss or muscle gain, stick to one unit system. Switching between pounds and kilograms creates false impressions. Losing 1 kg (2.2 lbs) sounds less dramatic than losing 2.2 lbs, even though they are identical.

Travel and Luggage

Airlines use kilograms for baggage limits almost everywhere except the US, where pounds are standard. A 23 kg checked bag limit equals 50.7 lbs. If you are flying from New York to London, your airline might quote 23 kg at check-in while your home scale shows pounds. Converting before you pack prevents expensive overweight fees at the airport.

Choose the Correct Reference

A conversion is only meaningful when the starting unit is known. Pounds, kilograms, ounces, and grams measure mass in ordinary use, while a force such as newtons describes weight in physics. For cooking and shipping, use the unit printed on the recipe or carrier form. The relationships include 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams, 1 pound = 16 ounces, and 1 kilogram is approximately 2.20462 pounds. Keep extra decimals because the pound relationship is not a short whole number.

Worked Kitchen and Shipping Examples

To convert 2.5 kg of flour to grams, calculate 2.5 × 1,000 = 2,500 g. To convert 5 lb to kilograms, calculate 5 ÷ 2.20462 = 2.26796 kg, commonly written as 2.27 kg. A shipping label may need the package weight rounded up according to carrier rules, while a recipe may need a practical kitchen measurement. Do not confuse a fluid ounce, which measures volume, with an ounce of mass.

Accuracy and Safe Rounding

  • Use a scale suited to the object and place it on a stable surface.
  • Zero or tare the container before measuring ingredients.
  • Keep the unrounded value until the final step, especially when combining shipments.
  • For medication, laboratory work, or regulated trade, follow the official specification.

Common Questions

How many ounces are in a pound? Sixteen avoirdupois ounces. Is a kilogram exactly 2.2 pounds? It is close, but 2.20462 is more precise. Why can two scales disagree? Calibration, surface level, battery condition, and resolution can matter. Use the weight converter to check arithmetic, then round only to the precision supported by your scale. For body measurements, pair verified weight with the BMI calculator and treat the result as a screening estimate.

Build a Conversion Record

For repeated work, write the date, original value, source unit, converted value, destination unit, and rounding rule. A record such as “12.4 lb ÷ 2.20462 = 5.625 kg, reported as 5.63 kg” is easier to audit than “about 5.6.” When several packages are combined, convert each item consistently before adding them. Do not mix a rounded package label with a high-precision scale reading without noting the difference.

Weight can change with moisture, packaging, and temperature, so a mathematical conversion cannot correct a poor measurement. Tare containers, keep liquids in a suitable vessel, and repeat a surprising reading. For regulated, medical, or safety-critical work, use calibrated equipment and the unit required by the responsible professional. The online converter is excellent for arithmetic, but it is not a substitute for that measurement procedure.

Reading a Weight Conversion Clearly

Always write the source and destination units beside a result. A value of 2.5 kilograms is 2,500 grams, about 5.51156 pounds, and about 88.185 ounces. The number is incomplete without its unit. For shipping, compare the carrier’s billed-weight rule with the scale reading; dimensional weight may be calculated from package length, width, and height instead of mass.

Worked kitchen and shipping examples

To convert 750 grams to kilograms, divide by 1,000: 750 ÷ 1,000 = 0.75 kg. To convert 12 pounds to kilograms, multiply by 0.45359237: 12 × 0.45359237 = 5.443 kg. If a recipe calls for 1.2 kg and your scale displays pounds, multiply 1.2 by 2.20462262 to get about 2.646 lb. Keep extra decimals until the final measurement.

Precision, Rounding, and Tare

Kitchen ingredients can often be rounded to the nearest gram, while laboratory or medication measurements require the precision specified by a professional. Tare the container before weighing, keep the scale on a stable surface, and repeat a reading that changes unexpectedly. Do not round each ingredient before adding a recipe total. A small error repeated across many packages can affect a shipping threshold.

Practical mistakes to avoid

  • Do not treat pounds and ounces as decimal pounds: 6 lb 8 oz is 6.5 lb, not 6.08 lb.
  • Check whether “ton” means a short ton, metric tonne, or long ton.
  • Separate mass from volume; a cup of flour and a cup of water do not weigh the same.
  • Record whether a package includes its box or wrapping.
