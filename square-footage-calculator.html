How to Calculate Square Footage

Square footage measures the surface area in square feet. It is widely used in real estate, flooring, painting, and construction projects.

Area (Square Feet) = Length (feet) × Width (feet)

If your measurements include inches, convert the inches to a decimal fraction of a foot by dividing the number of inches by 12 (for example, 6 inches = 0.5 feet).

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert square feet to square meters?

Multiply your square footage by 0.092903 to get the total area in square meters.

What if my room is not a perfect rectangle?

Divide the irregular space into separate rectangular zones, calculate the square footage of each zone, and add them together.

Measure Area Step by Step

Enter length and width in matching units. For a rectangle, area equals length × width. If a room is irregular, split it into rectangles, calculate each one, and add them. Convert inches to feet first: 30 inches is 2.5 feet. The calculator can help compare square feet, square meters, and square yards, but the quality of the answer depends on the measurements.

Worked example and allowance

A room 12 feet by 10 feet contains 120 square feet. If the material plan includes 10% waste, multiply by 1.10 to get 132 square feet for ordering. Waste covers cuts and breakage; it is not extra floor. Diagonal patterns, many corners, and fragile materials may need a different allowance recommended by the installer or manufacturer.

Openings and common mistakes

Subtract an opening only when it will not receive the material. For wall paint, calculate each wall’s length × height and subtract doors or windows when appropriate. Do not mix feet and inches, round each small section before adding, or assume product coverage is identical to measured area. Record every section in a worksheet and measure older walls twice.

Limitations and related tools

Area does not tell you the number of cans, cartons, or rolls without the manufacturer’s coverage. Read the coverage, number of coats, and package size before purchasing. For tile counts, use the tile calculator. The square footage guide includes irregular-room examples and measurement checks.

Create a purchasing worksheet

List every rectangle with its location, dimensions, unit, area, and material. Measure at the longest and shortest points when a wall is uneven, and use the larger realistic value for ordering. Keep openings documented instead of silently removing them. After calculating total area, multiply by the chosen allowance, check manufacturer coverage, and round up cartons or containers. This record helps resolve differences between a measured estimate and a supplier’s package count.

For paint, coverage depends on coats, porosity, texture, and color change. For flooring, transitions and cuts affect the order. For landscaping, slope and drainage may affect the usable surface. Ask the installer or manufacturer to review unusual shapes before purchase.

Area Formula and Worked Example

For a rectangle, area = length × width. A room measuring 14 feet by 11 feet has 154 square feet. For an L-shaped room, divide the outline into two non-overlapping rectangles, calculate each area, and add them. Measure in the same unit throughout; feet multiplied by inches do not produce square feet until the inches are converted.

Irregular rooms and waste

Sketch each section and label inside dimensions. Subtract a closet or permanent opening only when its dimensions are measured accurately, but do not subtract movable furniture. For flooring, multiply the net area by 1.10 for a modest cutting allowance, then round up to the supplier’s package size. Diagonal patterns, many corners, and future repairs can justify 15% or more.

Practical Mistakes and FAQs

Do not confuse perimeter with area, and do not add all sides when the task asks for coverage. Recheck whether a plan uses feet, inches, meters, or centimeters. What if a room is 12 feet 6 inches long? Convert to 12.5 feet before multiplying. How can I verify a result? Estimate the size, calculate a second way, and compare with the square footage guide. For tile quantity, continue to the tile calculator.

A Reliable Measuring Workflow

Measure length and width at two points and use the smaller clearance when a wall is uneven. Label each dimension with its unit before entering it. For a ceiling, wall, or paint project, distinguish square coverage from linear trim and perimeter. Keep a sketch with doors, windows, cabinets, and columns so the supplier can review the estimate.

Ordering check

After adding waste, divide by the coverage per box and round up. Compare the total with the room’s rough dimensions; a dramatic difference usually indicates mixed units or a duplicated section.

Area for Materials

For paint, flooring, turf, or roofing, confirm whether the product is sold by square foot, square yard, square meter, roll, or box. Convert all sections to the selling unit before ordering. Note ceilings, alcoves, stairs, and transitions separately because their installation waste can differ.

Worked check

A 10 by 12 rectangle is 120 square feet. Two such rectangles total 240 square feet, not 22 square feet. Write a unit after every intermediate result to prevent this common mistake.

Measure area before buying materials

Square footage describes two-dimensional area, not perimeter or volume. For a rectangle, multiply length by width in feet. A room that is 12 ft by 15 ft has 180 square feet. If measurements are in inches, convert each side to feet first by dividing by 12; 144 in by 120 in is 12 ft by 10 ft, or 120 square feet.

Irregular rooms and worked planning

Split an L-shaped floor into two rectangles, calculate each area, then add them. For example, sections measuring 10 by 12 and 6 by 5 total 120 + 30 = 150 square feet, provided the sections do not overlap. For a triangular section, use one-half times base times height. A circle uses pi times radius squared. Measure the radius from the center, and keep all dimensions in the same unit.

How to use the result

  1. Sketch the space and record each wall measurement at least twice.
  2. Convert inches, centimeters, or meters before multiplying.
  3. Calculate separate spaces instead of forcing an irregular outline into one rectangle.
  4. Add a practical waste allowance after calculating net area.

Waste, assumptions, and mistakes

Tile, laminate, paint, and carpet rarely cover a space with zero waste. A common planning allowance is 10%, with more for diagonal layouts, many corners, patterned tile, or inexperienced cutting. Multiplying the net area by 1.10 gives a 10% allowance: 150 square feet becomes 165 square feet to order. Do not add waste separately to overlapping sections, and do not confuse square feet with linear feet. Should closets count? Include areas that receive the same material, then subtract permanent openings when appropriate. Can I mix metric and imperial values? Convert first. For material planning, compare this answer with the manufacturer's coverage and the tile calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does square footage mean?

Square footage is the area of a surface measured in square feet.

Can I calculate irregular rooms?

Yes, when the room can be divided into simpler shapes whose areas can be calculated separately.

Should I add extra material?

For flooring and similar projects, a project-specific waste allowance is often useful.

Why can my contractor total differ?

Quotes can include waste, cuts, installation constraints, thresholds, or areas that a simple room measurement excludes.
