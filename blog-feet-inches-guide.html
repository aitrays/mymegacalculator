How to Convert Feet and Inches to Centimeters: Complete Guide

By Alex Morgan · August 2026 · 7 min read

If you have ever filled out a medical form abroad, bought furniture from a European retailer, or tried to explain your height to someone using the metric system, you have faced the imperial-to-metric conversion challenge. Feet and inches are intuitive for those raised in the US, UK, and Canada, but the rest of the world operates in centimeters and meters. This guide will teach you the exact formulas, provide quick-reference charts, and explain why accurate height and length conversions matter in medicine, travel, sports, and everyday life.

The Exact Conversion Formulas

The relationship between imperial and metric length units is defined by international agreement. These are not approximations — they are exact standards used in science, engineering, and medicine worldwide.

1 Foot = 30.48 cm (exact)
1 Inch = 2.54 cm (exact)
1 Foot = 12 Inches

To convert any feet-and-inches measurement to centimeters, use this simple two-step process:

  1. Convert feet to centimeters: Feet × 30.48
  2. Convert inches to centimeters: Inches × 2.54
  3. Add the two results together

Step-by-Step Example: 5 Feet 9 Inches

Let us convert one of the most common heights — 5 feet 9 inches — to centimeters:

5 feet × 30.48 = 152.40 cm
9 inches × 2.54 = 22.86 cm
Total = 152.40 + 22.86 = 175.26 cm

This means someone who is 5'9" is approximately 175 cm tall. In meters, that is 1.75 m.

Why Precision Matters

You might wonder why we bother with decimals like 175.26 cm instead of just saying "about 175 cm." In most casual conversations, rounding is fine. But in several contexts, precision is critical:

  • Medical records: Doctors track growth, BMI, and medication dosages using exact measurements. A 2 cm error in height can shift a child's growth percentile.
  • Passport applications: Many countries require height in centimeters. An incorrect entry can cause processing delays.
  • Sports and fitness: Boxing, gymnastics, and rowing use weight classes and equipment sizing that depend on exact body dimensions.
  • Construction and furniture: A sofa that is 2 cm too wide for a doorway is a sofa that will not fit.

Reverse Conversion: Centimeters to Feet and Inches

What if you know your height in centimeters and need to convert back to feet and inches? Here is the process:

  1. Divide centimeters by 30.48 to get total feet (including decimal).
  2. The whole number is the feet portion.
  3. Multiply the decimal portion by 12 to get the remaining inches.

Example: 170 cm ÷ 30.48 = 5.577 feet
Feet = 5
Inches = 0.577 × 12 = 6.93 inches
Result = 5 feet 6.9 inches (approximately 5'7")

Reading Measurements Correctly

Notation can cause as many errors as arithmetic. The mark before a number, such as 5' 9", means five feet and nine inches; it does not mean 5.9 feet. A decimal measurement is different: 5.5 feet equals 5 feet 6 inches because 0.5 × 12 = 6. If a form asks for centimeters, convert the complete measurement rather than treating the inches as a decimal. For a measuring tape, keep it level, remove shoes when measuring height, and record the result before rounding.

Choosing a Useful Rounding Level

Keep two decimal places while checking the calculation, then round according to the job. A medical or equipment form may request the nearest centimeter, while a doorway, countertop, or garment pattern may need millimeters. For 175.26 cm, the nearest centimeter is 175 cm and the nearest millimeter is 1,752.6 mm. Do not round each component before adding. State the unit beside every final number.

Three Practical Checks

  • Estimate first: one foot is a little over 30 cm, so 6 feet should be near 183 cm.
  • Check total inches: 5 feet 9 inches is (5 × 12) + 9 = 69 inches, and 69 × 2.54 = 175.26 cm.
  • Reverse the result: divide 175.26 by 2.54 to return to 69 inches.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use height with shoes? Usually no; official height is measured barefoot. Can I convert only inches? Yes. Multiply total inches by 2.54. Is 1.8 meters the same as 6 feet? It is close, but 6 feet is 182.88 cm. What about 3/8 inch? Convert the fraction to decimal inches, then multiply by 2.54. Compare manual work with the feet and inches calculator, and use the BMI calculator only when you also have a current body weight.

Using Conversions for Real Decisions

When buying clothing, compare the garment chart with your body measurement rather than assuming a labeled size is universal. For furniture, measure the narrowest doorway, lift, and hallway turn, then compare those dimensions in centimeters with the product dimensions. For travel forms, copy the converted number carefully and keep the original feet-and-inches value in your notes. In a clinic, use the measuring method requested by the clinician because posture, time of day, and equipment can affect a height reading.

A useful habit is to write a conversion as an equation before entering it into a tool: (feet × 12 + inches) × 2.54 = centimeters. This single equation gives the same answer as the two-part method and makes the order of operations visible. If the inch field contains a fraction, convert it first; 7 1/2 inches is 7.5 inches. If the result is being used for a safety clearance, leave a margin rather than designing to the exact converted value.

Measurement Context Matters

Feet and inches are used for more than height. In a renovation, record the length, width, and clearance separately so a conversion does not hide a fit problem. A 6-foot board may fit through a 72-inch opening on paper, but diagonal movement, trim, and hand space can reduce the usable clearance. For clothing, compare the measurement method used by the size chart with your own tape measure.

Worked fraction example

Suppose a shelf is 4 feet 7 1/2 inches long. Convert the feet first: 4 × 12 = 48 inches. Add 7.5 inches to get 55.5 inches. Multiplying by 2.54 gives 140.97 centimeters. Keeping the half inch as 0.5 rather than writing “7.5 feet” prevents a common unit error. Reverse the calculation by dividing 140.97 by 2.54 and check that it returns 55.5 inches.

Rounding for Forms and Construction

Choose the final precision from the decision being made. A passport form may ask for whole centimeters, while a cut list may need the nearest 1/16 inch. Never round the feet and inches separately before converting the total. Write the original measurement beside the converted one, and label whether it is an inside dimension, outside dimension, or clearance.

FAQ and next steps

Is 5.9 feet five feet nine inches? No. 5.9 decimal feet equals 5 feet 10.8 inches. What is the fastest check? Convert to total inches, then multiply by 2.54. How should I handle a fraction? Turn the fraction into decimal inches before using the formula.
